How to choose Chinese songs for your HSK level

The HSK is the standard Chinese proficiency test, and its vocabulary lists give a useful yardstick for songs too. A song written mostly with HSK 1–3 characters is a gentle way into listening; one full of rare characters will be frustrating. Here is how to judge a song before you invest time in it.

A quick note on HSK 3.0

The current standard (HSK 3.0) groups vocabulary into levels from 1 to 9. Levels 1 to 6 are the familiar ones; 7–9 are advanced. PinyinLyrics colours characters in lyrics using these HSK 3.0 levels, with levels 7–9 shown as a single group.

What makes lyrics easy

  • Repetition. Choruses that repeat the same lines give you free practice.
  • Everyday vocabulary. Love, family, weather, travel and daily life words are common in the lower levels.
  • Slow tempo and clear singing. Easier to follow while reading.
  • Short lines. Less to parse at once.

What makes lyrics hard

  • Poetic or classical vocabulary (chengyu, literary references, rare characters).
  • Fast rap with few pauses.
  • Slang and dialect that dictionaries don't cover well.

Use colour to read a song's difficulty

Turn on HSK colouring in the lyrics and look at the first verse. If most characters are in the colours of your level or below, the song is a good fit. A few characters from the next level are ideal: that's the vocabulary you're ready to learn. A sea of uncoloured characters (outside the HSK lists) usually means the song is too specialised for now.

A simple method

  1. Open a song you like and enable HSK colours.
  2. Count roughly how many characters are above your level in the first verse.
  3. If it's a handful, go ahead. If it's most of the line, save the song for later.
  4. Learn the new characters from the chorus first. They'll come back many times.

Keep a list of favourites by difficulty

Save songs as favourites to open their lyrics later without a connection. A short list of songs at the right level is more useful than a long list you'll never get through.
